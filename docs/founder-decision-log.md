**Where an earlier decision's rationale argues against these, and what was built**

- **38 vs decision 3 (no reactivation, "avoid a casual reversal path"):** reactivation is administrator-only, needs a reason and a confirmation that says what returns and what doesn't, and is audit-logged. It restores sign-in and the organization role only; former household assignments and owned work are **not** restored, so nothing reappears silently.
- **38/39 vs decision 2 (`staff.lifecycle.manage` operations-only as a sensitive power):** role changes and reactivation now need the administrator role. Deactivation stays with operations too, because removing access can't expand anyone's authority. Nobody can change their own role or reactivate themselves.
- **39 vs the 24-hour line being an operations setting:** the 24-hour line and email sender name are organization settings, so they move to administrators. Operations keep the reply time and routine-message wording (messaging templates), so decision 29b's prompt to operations still makes sense.

### Decision 63 in full (2026-10-22)

**1. The approved rule.**

- A person's name means a person wrote the content.
- Family-visible content posted or assembled automatically says so.
- Automatic content never appears under a person's name, and is never labelled "Your care team"
  as if a person wrote it.
- Automatic content uses no assistant-style branding, no first-person software voice ("I", "we")
  and no human-like language.
- Automatic summaries assembled from existing records say they were put together automatically,
  and reference their sources where appropriate.
- "Ask about this" may still route to people on the care team.
- Every future family-visible automatic content follows this rule. It doesn't cover messages or
  updates a person writes (they keep the person's name), or drafts a person sends after preparing
  them with help (register R-02).

The approved pattern (option C in the decision brief): a quiet tag, "Automatic notice" for content
Zeporah posts or "Put together automatically" for content it assembles, plus a source line where a
person's name would go: "Sent automatically for your care team", or for summaries "Made from your
household's updates", with links to sources. Never a name, initials or picture.

**2. The narrow amendment to decision 62.** The temporary cover notice is attributed with the
approved pattern: the "Automatic notice" tag and "Sent automatically for your care team", instead
of "Your care team". Nothing else in decision 62 changes: its timing (when cover becomes active,
first load after the start, once per cover), audience (coordinator, proxies with an active
authority workflow, the patient when they see updates), delivery (in-app only), body copy, audit
behaviour (`update.cover_notice_posted`, actor System, staff-visible) and the rule of no household
access before the start (decision 59).

**3. Rationale.** Families trust a name next to a message to mean a person chose to say it. The
cover notice showed "Your care team" though Zeporah posted it, while the care team's own record
marked it System, so the two sides told different stories and a family could reply to someone who
never wrote it. Saying plainly that content is automatic, and sent for the care team, keeps the
hospice relationship without pretending a person spoke and without turning the software into a
character. That keeps the family experience calm and honest as more automatic content arrives.

**4. After the freeze lifts.** Not built yet; the frozen demo baseline is unchanged except for
this record. When you lift the freeze for it:

- Replace the "Your care team" fallback for updates without an author in the two family services
  that build Home and Messages (`family.ts`), and show the tag and source line on Home and in
  Messages.
- Add tests: the notice shows the tag and source line and never "Your care team" or a person's
  name; updates a person writes still show their name.
- Refresh run-through screenshot 22 and rebuild the baseline manifest. Daniel's Home pixel test is
  unaffected (its data has no cover notice).
- Any later automatic content, such as status cards, "Since you last looked" or "Getting ready for
  a conversation", is built to this rule from the start and cites decision 63.
